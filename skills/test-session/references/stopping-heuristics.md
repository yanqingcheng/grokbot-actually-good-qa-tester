# When to stop testing: stopping heuristics

Used by step 10 of [run-test-session](../../run-test-session/SKILL.md). Applies to a single test, a probe group or a whole session, whether the tester is a person or an agent.

> **Credit:** These heuristics are from **Michael Bolton** (DevelopSense), who developed them with **James Bach** in Rapid Software Testing. Dale Emery and James Lyndsay contributed to the discussion. **Mission Rejected** was suggested by **Cem Kaner**, and the split of "Pause That Refreshes" into "Change in Priorities" and "Lights are Off" was suggested by **Joe Harter**. Sources: "When Do We Stop a Test?" (developsense.com/blog/2009/09/when-do-we-stop-test), "When Do We Stop Testing? One More Sure Thing" (developsense.com/blog/2009/10/when-do-we-stop-testing-one-more-sure), the talk slides "Why Does Testing Take So Long?" (developsense.com/presentations/2009-06-WhenDoWeStopTesting.pdf) and "Signing Off" (developsense.com/blog/2018/03/signing-off). The names are Bolton's. The meanings are paraphrased here, with short quotes only. "How this pack applies it" is our adaptation; any errors are ours.

## Read this first

- **They are heuristics:** fallible rules of thumb that need judgement and depend on context. Bolton pairs each one with a reason to question it, and so does this list. Pick the one that fits, and ask its question before you rely on it.
- **"Testing doesn't stop on its own … we stop testing when we decide to stop testing"** (Bolton). Stopping is a decision, so say which heuristic you used.
- **This pack's rules still apply.** The timebox is an upper limit. A stop can rest on a judgement under uncertainty, as long as you state the uncertainty. **You may stop on your own reasoned judgement** (step 10 of run-test-session), and you tell the manager straight away with the stopping reason, what was covered and the remaining risk. When being stuck or missing something could be fixed by a resource or a change of scope, pausing to ask is preferred ("Plan and get resources" in [test-session](../../test-session/SKILL.md)), but not required.
- **You inform; the manager or PO decides.** In Bolton's words, the release decision is "a business decision, not a technical one". Testers never sign off on the quality of the product. You may stand behind your account of the testing: what you found, what you covered and didn't, and what got in the way. Stopping testing is never a statement that the product is good enough.
- **Whatever the reason, record it.** In `notes.md` at the time, and in the report's **"Stopped because"** line, write the heuristic you used, why it applied, what wasn't covered and what risk remains.

## The heuristics

| Heuristic (Bolton's name) | What it means (paraphrased) | Question it before you rely on it | How this pack applies it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time's Up!** | The time allotted for testing has run out. | Did you get the information you needed? Is stopping now risky? Was the deadline arbitrary? | The timebox is an upper limit, not a target. When it runs out, stop, and give the manager what's left as follow-up work. Don't stretch the timebox yourself; ask for more time if the risk calls for it. |
| **Piñata** | Stop "when the candy starts falling out": that is, when you find the first sufficiently dramatic problem. | Is there "more candy" inside? Is the first dramatic problem the most important, or really dramatic at all? | **Report the dramatic problem to the manager straight away, before deciding anything else.** Then carry on with work it doesn't affect, or stop and tell the manager why, and record which. |
| **Dead Horse** | The product is too buggy for more testing to be worth it, or changes coming soon will invalidate it. | Might something even more important turn up? Are all the failures one underlying issue? | Stop if the evidence supports it, and tell the manager straight away with that evidence (the bugs, or the planned change) and the remaining risk. "It's probably all broken" is an unproven assumption. |
| **Mission Accomplished** | The questions you set out to answer are answered. | Has the testing raised new questions? Have enough known unknowns become known, and any new ones been named? | This is "mission covered" in step 10: the mission's questions answered as well as the evidence allows, the risks that matter explored in proportion, with sampling and gaps stated. New questions inside the charter's scope are part of the mission, so weigh them before you stop; any you leave go to the report as gaps or follow-up work, with why. It's your call: tell the manager straight away and report it in the "Stopped because" line; it is never a sign-off, and the manager can reopen the session. |
| **Mission Revoked** | The client (for you, the manager) says stop: budget gone, project cancelled, or any other reason. | Does the manager know the value of continuing, or the risk of stopping? Do you know their business reasons? | Stop. If you see important reasons to continue, tell the manager once, plainly, with evidence. It's their call. |
| **Mission Rejected** (Kaner) | You decline to continue because carrying on would cause a problem for someone, especially an ethical one (Bolton's examples include faking results and causing harm). | Is the problem real, and have you said it out loud? | Stop that work and tell the manager why; this is the "mission rejected" ending in step 10 (the session ends if the rejection covers the whole mission, otherwise unaffected in-scope work carries on). This is never quiet; it sits alongside the hard lines and the risky-probe gate. |
| **I Feel Stuck!** | Something seems to block you: missing information, a blocking bug, missing tools, equipment or expertise. | Could help, a workaround, another path, or simply asking get you unstuck? Bolton: "There's a difference between feeling stuck and being stuck." | Prove what's missing, or record it as unverified. **Pausing to ask is preferred** when a resource or a narrower charter could unblock you; carry on with work that doesn't depend on it. You may instead stop, on your judgement: tell the manager straight away what blocked you, what was covered and the remaining risk. Don't present an unverified "can't" as proven: say what you tried and how sure you are. |
| **Pause That Refreshes** (with **Change in Priorities** and **Lights are Off**, after Harter) | Suspend rather than stop: to rest, research, plan or reflect, or because something else matters more for now. "Lights are Off" means going through the motions without fresh ideas (Bolton links it to Parkinson's law). | Would carrying on now teach you more? Is the other priority real, and ready? | A pause is not a stop. Log it with the time. Time spent waiting (for a resource, a lease or a decision) doesn't count against the timebox; time spent on research, planning or reflection does, because it is part of testing (as in run-test-session step 10). If you're out of ideas, change approach (another heuristic, tour or setting) rather than repeating old checks; if that doesn't help, it may be Mission Accomplished or No More Interesting Questions, so say which. |
| **Flatline** | Whatever you do, you get the same result: the product has crashed or stopped responding, or it's very stable. | Is it really down, or recovering? Is no response itself a finding? Did "no matter what" include enough variation and load? | Write down what you varied. A crash or hang is a finding: report it. Stop that line when more variation no longer seems worth its cost, saying what you varied and what you didn't; if that ends the session, tell the manager as step 10 says. |
| **Customary Conclusion** | You stop where you usually stop: a set number of cases, cycles or checks (for example "when all the acceptance tests pass"). | Why do you always stop here? Would support, sales or reviewers suggest otherwise? Have you considered the other heuristics? | This is never enough on its own in this pack: passed checks are not success. Use it only together with Mission Accomplished, and say so. |
| **Change Tack** | Stop this line of testing and switch to a different approach. | Is the new tack better for the mission, or just new? | Record the switch and why. It is a stop for one line of testing, not for the session. |
| **No More Interesting Questions** | No remaining question is worth the cost of answering it. Bolton notes this one "tends to inform the others". | How good is your risk model? Is a Black Swan, or an ignored White Swan, waiting? Is coverage enough? Have your oracles been checked? | Name the questions you considered and why each isn't worth more time now. It can end the session on its own: that call is yours, made with that evidence; tell the manager straight away with what was covered and the remaining risk, and report it in the "Stopped because" line. If you're not sure, Change Tack first; it is never a sign-off, and the manager can reopen the session. |
| **Cost vs. Value** | Bolton calls this one the foundation of all the others: stop when continuing costs more than the information is worth. | Are you still finding information worth the cost? Is there a cheaper or better alternative? | Say what more testing would cost and what it might find. A cost to the owner that wasn't agreed as one of the session's resources at readiness ("Plan and get resources" in [test-session](../../test-session/SKILL.md)) is a reason to stop and tell the manager; spend that was agreed there is fine (illustration: a paid API's calls were agreed for the core flow, but exploring further would start charging for a second service nobody agreed: stop that line and tell the manager). |
| **Avoidance/Indifference** | Someone doesn't want or need more information: a throwaway build, release reasons that no finding would change, or a lack of motivation. | If nobody cares, why test at all? Have priorities been lost? Is someone avoiding knowing about a problem? | If you see it in others, tell the manager what you see. If you notice it in yourself, treat it as a prompt to reassess: change tack, or, if you judge that continuing really lacks value, stop and tell the manager straight away with why and the remaining risk. |

## Recording a stop

Write it in `notes.md` when it happens, then in the report's "Stopped because" line:

`Stopped because: <heuristic> — <why it applied, with evidence> · Not covered: <what> · Risk remaining: <what and for whom> · Manager informed: <when (straight away)>`

When you stop only one line of investigation and the session carries on, note it in `notes.md` (Change Tack); ending the session always means telling the manager straight away.

Illustration: `Stopped because: Mission Accomplished — the mission's risks explored on desktop and phone, sampling in notes 14:02–14:40 · Not covered: speech on phone (voices unverified; asked the manager 14:05, manager agreed 14:12 to drop it from this charter, notes 14:12) · Charter change: phone speech out of scope, agreed 14:12 · Risk remaining: phone visitors may hear the wrong voice · Manager informed: 14:41`.
