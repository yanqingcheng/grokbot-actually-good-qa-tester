# Stopping heuristics

Used by "Before you stop" in [run-test-session](../../run-test-session/SKILL.md). Applies to a test, a probe group or a session.

> Credit: Michael Bolton's stopping heuristics (DevelopSense), developed with James Bach in Rapid Software Testing. Mission Rejected was suggested by Cem Kaner, and the split of "Pause That Refreshes" into "Change in Priorities" and "Lights are Off" by Joe Harter. Sources: "When Do We Stop a Test?" (developsense.com/blog/2009/09/when-do-we-stop-test), "When Do We Stop Testing? One More Sure Thing" (developsense.com/blog/2009/10/when-do-we-stop-testing-one-more-sure), "Why Does Testing Take So Long?" (developsense.com/presentations/2009-06-WhenDoWeStopTesting.pdf) and "Signing Off" (developsense.com/blog/2018/03/signing-off). Dale Emery and James Lyndsay contributed to the discussion. Names are Bolton's. The "Our rule" column is AGQT policy, not Bolton's.

Open this before you decide to stop. Each heuristic is fallible: ask its question before you rely on it, and name the one you used. Stopping is never a statement that the product is good enough. Never sign off on quality ("The tester informs, the manager decides" in [write-test-report](../../write-test-report/SKILL.md)). Meanings and questions are paraphrased from Bolton. The "Our rule" column is AGQT policy. How to notify and pause: "Before you stop" in [run-test-session](../../run-test-session/SKILL.md).

| Heuristic | Meaning (Bolton) | Ask first | Our rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time's Up! | The charter's timebox (if any) has run out. | Did you get the information you needed? Is stopping now risky? | Applies only to a timebox in this charter, checked with `date`; never one from an earlier round or memory. Report by it and hand the rest over as follow-up work. Ask for more time if the risk calls for it. |
| Piñata | Stop when the candy falls out: the first sufficiently dramatic problem. | Is it the most important problem, or really dramatic? Is more candy inside? | Send the bug progress report ("Progress reports" in run-test-session) straight away. Then carry on with unaffected work, or stop and say why. Record which. |
| Dead Horse | Too buggy for more testing to be worth it, or coming changes will invalidate it. | Might something more important turn up? Are the failures one underlying cause? | Stop only if evidence supports it, citing the bugs or the planned change. "It's probably all broken" is an unproven assumption. |
| Mission Accomplished | The questions you set out to answer are answered. | Has testing raised new questions? Were the mission's risks explored in proportion, with sampling and gaps stated? | Before claiming it, list in `notes.md` each focus the PO or manager named and each scope addition: explored (with evidence) or a gap with its reason. Not while the mission's core is untested, or a PO answer it depends on is pending. No coverage quota. New in-scope questions are part of the mission: weigh them first. The manager can reopen. |
| Mission Revoked | The client (the manager) says stop. | Do they know the value of continuing and the risk of stopping? | Stop. Record who and when. If you see important reasons to continue, tell the manager once, with evidence. It's their call. |
| Mission Rejected (Kaner) | You decline because continuing would cause a problem for someone, especially an ethical one. | Is the problem real, and have you said it out loud? | Never reject quietly ("Before you stop"). |
| I Feel Stuck! | Something seems to block you: information, a bug, tools, equipment or expertise. | Is it feeling stuck or being stuck? Could help, a workaround or asking unstick you? | Prove what's missing, or record it as unverified. Never present an unverified "can't" as proven. |
| Pause That Refreshes (Change in Priorities, Lights are Off, after Harter) | Suspend rather than stop: to rest, research or reflect, because something else matters more, or because you're going through the motions. | Would carrying on now teach you more? Is the other priority real? | A pause is not a stop. Log it with the time. Out of ideas: change approach before calling it Mission Accomplished or No More Interesting Questions. |
| Flatline | Whatever you do, the same result: crashed, hung, or very stable. | Is it really down, or recovering? Is no response a finding? Did you vary enough, including load? | A crash or hang is a finding. Stop that line when more variation isn't worth its cost, writing what you varied and didn't. |
| Customary Conclusion | You stop where you usually stop (a set number of checks, all acceptance checks passing). | Why here? Would support or reviewers say otherwise? Have you weighed the other heuristics? | Never enough alone: passed checks are not success. Use only with Mission Accomplished, and say so. |
| Change Tack | Stop this line and switch approach. | Is the new tack better for the mission, or just new? | Record the switch and why. It stops one line, not the session. |
| No More Interesting Questions | No remaining question is worth its cost. It tends to inform the others. | How good is your risk model? Is a Black Swan, or an ignored White Swan, waiting? Have your oracles been checked? | Name the questions considered and why each isn't worth more time. If unsure, Change Tack first. |
| Cost vs. Value | The foundation of the others: continuing costs more than the information is worth. | Is the information still worth the cost? Is there a cheaper alternative? | Say what more testing would cost and might find. An unagreed cost to the owner: "Before you stop". |
| Avoidance/Indifference | Someone doesn't want or need more information (a throwaway build, a fixed release decision, low motivation). | If nobody cares, why test? Is someone avoiding knowing about a problem? | In others: tell the manager what you see. In yourself: reassess, change tack, or stop if continuing really lacks value. |

## Recording a stop

Write it in `notes.md` when it happens, then copy it into the report:

`Stopped because: <heuristic>: <why it applied, with evidence> · Time used: <minutes> of <this charter's timebox, or "no timebox"> · Not covered: <what, with why> · Risk remaining: <what and for whom> · Next: <recommended small charter(s)> · Manager informed: <when (straight away)>`

Stopping one line while the session continues is a Change Tack note. Ending the session always means the stop progress report, straight away.

Illustration: `Stopped because: Mission Accomplished: the mission's risks explored on desktop and phone, sampling in notes 14:02–14:40 · Time used: 38 of 40 min · Not covered: speech on phone (voices unverified, asked 14:05, manager agreed 14:12 to drop it) · Charter change: phone speech out of scope, agreed 14:12 · Risk remaining: phone visitors may hear the wrong voice · Next: phone speech once voices are proven · Manager informed: 14:41`
