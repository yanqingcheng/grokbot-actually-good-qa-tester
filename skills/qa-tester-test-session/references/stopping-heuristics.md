# When to stop testing: stopping heuristics

Used by step 10 of [qa-run-test-session](../../qa-run-test-session/SKILL.md). Applies to a single test, a probe group or a whole session, whether the tester is a person or an agent.

> **Credit:** These heuristics are from **Michael Bolton** (DevelopSense), who developed them with **James Bach** in Rapid Software Testing. Dale Emery and James Lyndsay contributed to the discussion. **Mission Rejected** was suggested by **Cem Kaner**, and the split of "Pause That Refreshes" into "Change in Priorities" and "Lights are Off" was suggested by **Joe Harter**. Sources: "When Do We Stop a Test?" (developsense.com/blog/2009/09/when-do-we-stop-test), "When Do We Stop Testing? One More Sure Thing" (developsense.com/blog/2009/10/when-do-we-stop-testing-one-more-sure), the talk slides "Why Does Testing Take So Long?" (developsense.com/presentations/2009-06-WhenDoWeStopTesting.pdf) and "Signing Off" (developsense.com/blog/2018/03/signing-off). The names are Bolton's. The meanings are paraphrased here, with short quotes only. "How this pack applies it" is our adaptation; any errors are ours.

## Read this first

- **They are heuristics:** fallible rules of thumb that need judgement and depend on context. Bolton pairs each one with a reason to question it, and so does this list. Pick the one that fits, and ask its question before you rely on it.
- **"Testing doesn't stop on its own … we stop testing when we decide to stop testing"** (Bolton). Stopping is a decision, so say which heuristic you used.
- **This pack's rules still apply.** The timebox is an upper limit. Stopping on an unproven assumption is never allowed. Being stuck or missing something the mission needs means pause and ask, as the readiness rule says ("Plan and get resources" in [qa-tester-test-session](../../qa-tester-test-session/SKILL.md)).
- **You inform; the manager or PO decides.** In Bolton's words, the release decision is "a business decision, not a technical one". Testers never sign off on the quality of the product. You may stand behind your account of the testing: what you found, what you covered and didn't, and what got in the way. Stopping testing is never a statement that the product is good enough.
- **Whatever the reason, record it.** In `notes.md` at the time, and in the report's **"Stopped because"** line, write the heuristic you used, why it applied, what wasn't covered and what risk remains.

## The heuristics

| Heuristic (Bolton's name) | What it means (paraphrased) | Question it before you rely on it | How this pack applies it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Time's Up!** | The time allotted for testing has run out. | Did you get the information you needed? Is stopping now risky? Was the deadline arbitrary? | The timebox is an upper limit, not a target. When it runs out, stop, and give the manager what's left as follow-up work. Don't stretch the timebox yourself; ask for more time if the risk calls for it. |
| **Piñata** | Stop "when the candy starts falling out": that is, when you find the first sufficiently dramatic problem. | Is there "more candy" inside? Is the first dramatic problem the most important, or really dramatic at all? | **Report the dramatic problem to the manager straight away, before deciding anything else.** Then carry on with work it doesn't affect, or stop if the manager agrees, and record which. |
| **Dead Horse** | The product is too buggy for more testing to be worth it, or changes coming soon will invalidate it. | Might something even more important turn up? Are all the failures one underlying issue? | Show the evidence (the bugs, or the planned change) and ask the manager before stopping. "It's probably all broken" is an unproven assumption. |
| **Mission Accomplished** | The questions you set out to answer are answered. | Has the testing raised new questions? Have enough known unknowns become known, and any new ones been named? | This is "mission covered" in step 10: every in-scope action tried to its end at each target setting, with evidence, and depth spent where the risks call for it. New questions inside the charter's scope are part of the mission, so answer them while the timebox allows; a gap that needs a resource or a change of scope means pause and ask (step 10), not follow-up work. Only questions outside this charter's scope go to the report as follow-up work. It's your call, reported in the "Stopped because" line; it is never a sign-off, and the manager can reopen the session. |
| **Mission Revoked** | The client (for you, the manager) says stop: budget gone, project cancelled, or any other reason. | Does the manager know the value of continuing, or the risk of stopping? Do you know their business reasons? | Stop. If you see important reasons to continue, tell the manager once, plainly, with evidence. It's their call. |
| **Mission Rejected** (Kaner) | You decline to continue because carrying on would cause a problem for someone, especially an ethical one (Bolton's examples include faking results and causing harm). | Is the problem real, and have you said it out loud? | Stop that work and tell the manager why; this is the "mission rejected" ending in step 10 (the session ends if the rejection covers the whole mission, otherwise unaffected in-scope work carries on). This is never quiet; it sits alongside the hard lines and the risky-probe gate. |
| **I Feel Stuck!** | Something seems to block you: missing information, a blocking bug, missing tools, equipment or expertise. | Could help, a workaround, another path, or simply asking get you unstuck? Bolton: "There's a difference between feeling stuck and being stuck." | **Pause and ask**, as readiness says. Prove what's missing, or record it as unverified, ask the manager for it or for a narrower charter, and carry on with work that doesn't depend on it. Never stop on an unproven "can't", and never end the session on a block yourself: it ends only if the manager agrees or says stop. |
| **Pause That Refreshes** (with **Change in Priorities** and **Lights are Off**, after Harter) | Suspend rather than stop: to rest, research, plan or reflect, or because something else matters more for now. "Lights are Off" means going through the motions without fresh ideas (Bolton links it to Parkinson's law). | Would carrying on now teach you more? Is the other priority real, and ready? | A pause is not a stop. Log it with the time. Time spent waiting (for a resource, a lease or a decision) doesn't count against the timebox; time spent on research, planning or reflection does, because it is part of testing (as in qa-run-test-session step 10). If you're out of ideas, change approach (another heuristic, tour or setting) rather than repeating old checks; if that doesn't help, it may be Mission Accomplished or No More Interesting Questions, so say which. |
| **Flatline** | Whatever you do, you get the same result: the product has crashed or stopped responding, or it's very stable. | Is it really down, or recovering? Is no response itself a finding? Did "no matter what" include enough variation and load? | Write down what you varied. A crash or hang is a finding: report it. Stop that line only once your variation genuinely covers the risks; the session itself ends only in one of step 10's ways. |
| **Customary Conclusion** | You stop where you usually stop: a set number of cases, cycles or checks (for example "when all the acceptance tests pass"). | Why do you always stop here? Would support, sales or reviewers suggest otherwise? Have you considered the other heuristics? | This is never enough on its own in this pack: passed checks are not success. Use it only together with Mission Accomplished, and say so. |
| **Change Tack** | Stop this line of testing and switch to a different approach. | Is the new tack better for the mission, or just new? | Record the switch and why. It is a stop for one line of testing, not for the session. |
| **No More Interesting Questions** | No remaining question is worth the cost of answering it. Bolton notes this one "tends to inform the others". | How good is your risk model? Is a Black Swan, or an ignored White Swan, waiting? Is coverage enough? Have your oracles been checked? | Name the questions you considered and why each isn't worth more time now. It ends the session only together with Mission Accomplished (the mission is covered, with its evidence) or an end agreed with the manager; otherwise it means Change Tack, or pause and ask. That call is yours, made with that evidence and reported in the "Stopped because" line; it is never a sign-off, and the manager can reopen the session. |
| **Cost vs. Value** | Bolton calls this one the foundation of all the others: stop when continuing costs more than the information is worth. | Are you still finding information worth the cost? Is there a cheaper or better alternative? | Say what more testing would cost and what it might find, and let the manager weigh it. |
| **Avoidance/Indifference** | Someone doesn't want or need more information: a throwaway build, release reasons that no finding would change, or a lack of motivation. | If nobody cares, why test at all? Have priorities been lost? Is someone avoiding knowing about a problem? | Never a reason for you to stop on your own. If you see it in others, tell the manager what you see. If you see it in yourself, say so and pause, as above. |

## Recording a stop

Write it in `notes.md` when it happens, then in the report's "Stopped because" line:

`Stopped because: <heuristic> — <why it applied, with evidence> · Not covered: <what> · Risk remaining: <what and for whom> · Manager informed: <when, or "not needed">`

Illustration: `Stopped because: Mission Accomplished — every in-scope action tried on desktop and phone (notes 14:02–14:40) · Not covered: speech on phone (voices unverified; asked the manager 14:05, manager agreed 14:12 to drop it from this charter, notes 14:12) · Charter change: phone speech out of scope, agreed 14:12 · Risk remaining: phone visitors may hear the wrong voice · Manager informed: 14:41`.
